\opening{Dear Editors,}

We submit the manuscript ``Geometric Observables for Financial Regime
Detection'' for consideration as an original research article in
\emph{Quantitative Finance}.

The paper introduces four geometric detectors---Berry Phase Rate,
Spectral Entropy, Reduced State Purity, and Hamiltonian
Sensitivity---derived from a learned spectral embedding of equity-index
returns, and evaluates them against 46 classical and machine-learning
baselines (including the Absorption Ratio, Hamilton's Markov-switching
model, GARCH, Bayesian online changepoint detection, and supervised
Random Forests) on 17 historical crises spanning 2000--2024.  Under
walk-forward nested hyperparameter selection on nine labelled windows,
the Berry Phase Rate achieves an unbiased out-of-sample Cohen's
$d = 0.72$ (95\% percentile-bootstrap CI $[0.34,\,1.18]$) and reduces
the false-alarm rate by approximately 67\% relative to a label-supervised
Random Forest (1.2 vs.\ 3.6 alarms per year).  Geometric and classical
channels are largely uncorrelated (mean $|\rho| \approx 0.22$), suggesting the
observables provide a complementary risk-overlay signal rather than a
substitute for existing detectors.

Although the construction borrows formalism from quantum geometry, every
computation runs on classical hardware; the contribution is empirical
and methodological, not physical.  Statistical evaluation uses
Friedman tests with the Iman--Davenport correction, Nemenyi post-hoc
analysis, and percentile bootstrap confidence intervals.  All
experiments are reproducible from a public code release with pinned
random seeds and canonical result files.

For full transparency, a preprint of this manuscript is publicly
available on SSRN (\href{https://ssrn.com/abstract=6685298}{abstract
ID 6685298}); a corresponding arXiv posting is forthcoming.  The work
is original, has not appeared in any peer-reviewed venue, and is not
under consideration by any other journal.  We have no competing
interests to declare.

\closing{Sincerely,}
